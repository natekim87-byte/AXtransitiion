How to use the assessment

Steps

  1. Open tabs A through E and read the rule of thumb at the top of each.
  2. Score each question using the 1, 3, and 5 examples. If you're in between, pick 2 or 4.
  3. Check your level and bottleneck on the right.
  4. Pick your role, click Copy results, and send them to the AX lead by email or chat.

Scoring rules

  • Only count what you can show. If you can't point to a document, a budget line, or a screen, score it one level lower.
  • Score today, not the plan. "We're doing it next year" is a 1.
  • Not sure? Give it a 2 and flag it for the AX lead to confirm.
  • Score the whole company, not just your department.

Reading the results

  • Your level is the overall score rounded. 2.5–3.4 means L3.
  • Your level can't be more than one step above your weakest area. If the average says L4 but governance sits at L2, you land at L3.
  • A bottleneck is any area at least 0.5 points below the rest. Fix it before you scale.
  • If people's scores differ by 1.5 points or more on a question, put it on the Workshop 1 agenda.
STEP 2 · WORKSHOP 1

Set how far AI should go, by maturity

There are four levels of handing work to AI. Your company's maturity sets the ceiling. Within that ceiling, the nature of each task decides the actual level.

Maturity × automation level

MaturityRecommendedCeilingDo nowDon't do yet

Set the level for each task

Pick one task in a department and answer the questions below from the top. The level right before your first "no" is that task's level.

P1Can the person doing this task get help from an AI tool with drafts, summaries, or translation?Most office work qualifies.Yes → P1 is fine
P2Does the team repeat this task the same way, and can you give the AI the material it needs?Examples: a weekly report, proposals built from a standard templateYes → P2 is fine
P3Are the rules clear, and if the AI gets it wrong, is the damage small or easy for a person to catch?Examples: routing customer tickets, keying in invoice data. Pricing, contracts, and performance reviews don't qualify.Yes → P3 is fine
P4Are your data connections and controls strong enough to let AI make decisions across systems?Example: AI places replenishment orders based on its own demand forecastYes → P4 is fine
Then check your company's ceiling. Even if a task qualifies for P3, a company at L2 should stop at P2 for now. Without usage policies, IT support, and monitoring, there's no way to catch and fix errors once automation is running. When the company moves up a level, move the task up with it.

    Worked example

    Weekly sales briefing: get answers by asking your Tableau data

    A sales ops analyst spends four hours every Monday building the weekly sales briefing in Excel. Here's how to replace that with a handful of questions by connecting an LLM to the Tableau data you already have. Tableau's VizQL Data Service (VDS) pulls the data, Tableau's official MCP server connects it to the LLM, and Tableau Pulse flags unusual changes first.

    1Source dataPOS sales · e-commerce orders · B2B orders (ERP) · promo calendar · inventory (WMS)
    →
    2Tableau published data sourcePublished as one "Weekly_Sales_Combined" source, reusing what your dashboards already use
    →
    3VizQL Data ServiceTableau API that queries a published data source as JSON, no chart needed
    →
    4Tableau MCP serverExposes VDS and Pulse as tools an LLM can call
    ⇄
    5LLM (Claude, etc.)Understands the question, picks the right tools, queries, and explains the result
    Tableau Pulse Register weekly sales, average order value, online conversion, and B2B bookings as metrics, and Pulse automatically spots changes outside the normal range along with likely drivers. The LLM uses those findings as its starting point through MCP.

    ① What data to expose through VDS

    DataKey fieldsSourceUsed for
    SalesDate, store and region, channel (store/online/B2B), category, sales, units, transactionsPOS, e-commerce, ERPWeek-over-week and year-over-year change, by channel and region
    PromotionsDates, categories and stores, discountMarketing calendarExplaining swings (tough comps, etc.)
    InventoryDate, store, SKU, in-stock flagWMSEstimating sales lost to stockouts
    TargetsWeekly and monthly sales targetsFP&APercent to plan
    Add a one-line description to every field. Something like "net_sales: sales after discounts and returns" keeps the LLM from picking the wrong field. Documenting what your data means has the biggest effect on accuracy.

    ② Setup About 2 weeks, no development

    TaskWhoTime
    Clean up the published data source and add field descriptionsData team3–5 days
    Define four Pulse metrics (weekly sales, AOV, online conversion, B2B bookings)Sales ops + data team1 day
    Connect the Tableau MCP server to your LLM client. It uses a personal access token, so existing Tableau permissions still applyIT or AX lead1–2 days
    Save common questions and the briefing format as promptsSales ops (AI champion)1 day

    ③ Monday morning: a briefing built from questions Sample data

    How did we do last week? Anything move a lot?
    Pulse insight · weekly salesquery-datasource · region × channel, WoW and YoY
    Total sales last week were $3.62M, down 3.4% week over week and down 1.2% year over year. The one change Pulse flagged as outside the normal range was Northeast stores (−13.8%, −$218K). Online (+4.2%), other regions, and B2B were slightly up and offset part of the drop.
    Why were Northeast stores down? Break it out by category.
    query-datasource · Northeast stores × categoryquery-datasource · promotions
    Of the $218K drop, $157K (72%) came from outdoor. The same week last year had a 20%-off outdoor event in the Northeast, and there wasn't one this year. Hypothesis Most of the drop looks like a tough comp against last year's promotion.
    Did stockouts play a part?
    query-datasource · inventory (Northeast outdoor)
    3 of the top 20 outdoor SKUs were out of stock for 4+ days in the Northeast. At normal sell-through, that's roughly $31K in lost sales, about 20% of the outdoor decline.
    Give me a five-line summary for the store managers' call, plus what to follow up on this week.
    1. Total sales $3.62M, down 3.4% week over week
    2. The decline is concentrated in Northeast stores (−$218K); online up 4.2%
    3. 72% of the Northeast decline is outdoor: tough comp vs. last year's sale (hypothesis)
    4. Three outdoor SKUs out of stock, about $31K in lost sales
    5. B2B bookings within normal range
    Follow up this week: restock dates for the three SKUs, and whether to bring back the Northeast outdoor promotion

    The LLM shows which tool it called each time, so anyone can trace a number back to Tableau.

    Build the AI insights you want, without Palantir-style FDEs

    In the Palantir model, forward deployed engineers (FDEs) embed with your company and custom-build the data model and analytics apps. It's powerful, but it takes engineering time and budget, and every new question goes back to the engineers.

    Here, the Tableau published data source you already use acts as the data model, and the official MCP server handles the connection. Your team just changes the question to get the analysis they need. The point is that customizing the analysis moves from outside engineers to the people who do the work.

    FDE modelTableau + MCP + LLM
    Who builds itOutside engineers on siteYour data team + AX lead
    Time to startEngineer onboarding and build timeAbout 2 weeks
    New questionAsk the engineersYour team asks directly
    Data usedConsolidated into a new platformThe Tableau data sources you already have

    If you need to stitch together data from many systems and automate operational decisions end to end, an FDE-style platform may be the right fit. When the goal is analysis and insight, like a sales briefing, this approach is enough.

    Check before you commit. The Tableau MCP server is an open-source project from Tableau and is still changing. Whether you can use VDS and Pulse depends on your license and deployment (Tableau Cloud or Tableau Server). Query results go to the LLM, so use a business-tier LLM that doesn't train on your data.

      Appendix

      FAQ and glossary

      Our data is a mess. Can we still start?

      Yes. AI handles text like reviews, service tickets, and proposals well even before it's cleaned up. Don't wait for a data platform. Start with text-based work.

      Our teams are wary of AI.

      Change the message from "AI will replace jobs" to "AI takes the tedious parts off your plate." Pick first projects from the work people dislike most: reports, wrap-up notes, retyping data.

      Which AI tool should we use?

      Check security first: a business plan that doesn't train on your data, and how well it fits the collaboration tools you already use. Start with one tool, learn how people use it, then expand.

      We keep running PoCs that never scale.

      Set the go/no-go criteria and the operating owner before you start, and budget for the pilot when you approve the PoC.
